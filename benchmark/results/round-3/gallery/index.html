New. Kestrel clinic, reception check-in screen

Build the front desk screen for Kestrel Physio, our physiotherapy clinic. Use realistic sample data. Put it in this folder as index.html (plain HTML, CSS and JS, no build step).

The simulated client's private notes
# Kestrel Physio, front desk screen (client notes)

- Who uses it: our two receptionists, all day, on a 10-inch touch tablet in landscape at the desk. Both are in their 50s and 60s and not keen on technology. Big text and big buttons matter.
- Main jobs: check patients in when they arrive (one tap, the old system needed five), see today's appointments for our 4 physiotherapists, and spot who has been waiting longest.
- Also needed: add a walk-in, mark someone late or no-show, and see a small flag when a patient has a payment due.
- Feel: calm and light, like the clinic. Our brand colour is teal #2a7f7a. No dark mode.
- Pain with the old system: tiny controls, too many clicks, and you cannot see the waiting room at a glance.

New. Salt and Iron exhibition guide

Make the online guide for our exhibition Salt and Iron at the Harwick Maritime Museum. Put it in this folder as index.html (plain HTML, CSS and JS, no build step).

The simulated client's private notes
# Salt and Iron, Harwick Maritime Museum (client notes)

- How it is used: visitors scan a QR code at each of the 12 stops and read the guide on their own phone while standing in the gallery. Some stops are outdoors on the quay in bright light. Many visitors are over 60.
- So: large, high-contrast text, easy to move to the next and previous stop, and it should work with patchy signal.
- Each stop has a short text of about 120 words and an audio clip of 1 to 3 minutes.
- The 12 stops, in order: 1 The salt road; 2 Boiling houses; 3 The harbour in 1840; 4 Iron arrives; 5 The Harwick Lady; 6 Rivets and riveters; 7 A ship's salt store; 8 Smugglers and the excise; 9 The great storm of 1872; 10 Women of the quay; 11 Last of the salt boats; 12 What remains.
- Brand: the museum uses a rust red #9c3d1f with off-black and warm white. The printed gallery labels use a serif for headings, and the guide should feel like those labels.
- Exhibition dates: 14 March to 2 November 2027.
