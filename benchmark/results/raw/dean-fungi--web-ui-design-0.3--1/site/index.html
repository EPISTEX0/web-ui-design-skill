Do not eat a fungus on the strength of this guide.

Several of the deadliest fungi in Britain grow in the Forest of Dean, and they look like edible ones. Identification here is a starting point for the group’s recorder and experienced members, not a licence to cook.

    Try removing a filter, or loosen the month. If you are sure of the colour but not the rest, start with the colour alone. This guide covers a selection of common species, so yours may not be listed. Photograph it and take it to the recorder.

    Recording a find

    • Photograph the cap from above, the underside, and the whole stem.
    • Dig up the base rather than cutting it. A cup or sac down there can be the deciding feature.
    • Note the tree it was under, or the wood it grew on.
    • Write down the date and where you were. A grid reference helps.
    • For gilled species, lay the cap on paper overnight for a spore print.

    Picking in the Forest

    Forestry England sets the rules on picking fungi in the Forest of Dean, and some places are protected. Check their current guidance before you take anything. On group forays, leave what you do not need: fungi are the fruit of a much larger organism, and other wildlife depends on them.

    About this guide

    A selection of species commonly met on forays, not a full flora. Drawings are schematic, made to show shape and colour, and are not portraits. Colours vary a lot with age and weather. Check against a printed field guide, and ask the recorder if you are unsure.
